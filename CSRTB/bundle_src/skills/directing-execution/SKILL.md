# directing-execution — run a plan you already designed, and adapt it as it unfolds
# WHAT: the RUN-TIME half of orchestration. delegation-planning decides the distribution BEFORE dispatch; THIS drives it AFTER — supervise each wave, read what comes back, and choose continue / re-route / fix-first / abort / goal-met / adapt-the-plan. The director DISPATCHES and STEERS; it does not PERFORM the domain steps (those route to specialists).

## When to invoke
WHEN a plan is APPROVED and now EXECUTING and you are the lead directing it — especially under `/solo` ("proceed autonomously") — with sub-agents in flight and results landing. Concretely: you just dispatched a wave and must act on the results · a child died / stalled / returned garbage mid-run · a result changes what the next phase should be · you are tempted to spawn another cascade and must decide if it is warranted. NOT before dispatch (that is the design-time routing decision → `delegation-planning`); NOT the persona question (→ PLANNER profile).

## The signature error this prevents
**Thrash and drift under autonomy** — two run-time failures:
1. OVER-REACTION: re-routing, re-spawning cascades, or rewriting the plan on NOISE — a slow child, one weak result, a transient error — churning the orchestration without new information. Reflexive-cascade at run time.
2. UNDER-REACTION / DRIFT: mechanically running the approved plan to completion after a result has already invalidated a later phase — executing the letter of the plan past the point its purpose changed.
The fix is the same discipline as design-time: act on EVIDENCE, not on impulse or inertia — change course exactly when a result warrants it, and not otherwise.

## Load-bearing loop — supervise → decide → act (once per turn, at the wave boundary)
Direction is TURN-BOUNDARY reactive, not a background monitor: each turn you read what landed and choose the next action. Run this loop:
1. **HARVEST** — collect the results that are in. A blocking `host.delegate([...])` already returns them; for async waves use `host.collect([fids], timeout=…)` or let `wait_for_notification` wake you. Read `structured_output` + `artifacts_created` + `status` per child.
2. **ASSESS against the plan's PURPOSE** — not just "did the child finish" but "did it move the plan toward its goal". A child can complete and still return the wrong thing.
3. **DECIDE** one of six (criteria in the next section): CONTINUE (dispatch the next wave as planned) · RE-ROUTE (same plan, different owner/topology for the next step) · FIX-FIRST (a defect blocks the next wave — repair it before anything else proceeds) · ABORT (stop and surface, when the goal is unreachable or a gate needs the user) · GOAL-MET (terminal — update the ledgers, take the backup) · ADAPT (revise the plan artifact — add/drop/reorder phases — then continue).
4. **ACT + RECORD** — dispatch / steer / stop / edit-the-plan; update `update_step_status`; note *why* on any deviation so the trail explains the adaptation. Then end the turn (or dispatch the next wave and end).

## The six decisions — when each (the run-time WHICH)
- **CONTINUE** ⇒ results met their bar and the plan's next phase is still the right next step. Default; do NOT manufacture a course-change to look responsive.
- **RE-ROUTE** ⇒ the STEP still stands but its owner/topology was wrong (a specialist returned off-charter work, a single agent choked on what wants a fan-out, a fan-out was overkill). Change the how, keep the plan. Consult `delegation-planning` for the corrected routing.
- **FIX-FIRST** ⇒ a DEFECT the wave surfaced blocks the next one — a broken gate, a corrupted artifact, a wrong path baked into the remaining briefs. Repair it BEFORE anything else proceeds; a defect carried forward multiplies through every unit that consumes it. Distinct from RE-ROUTE (the routing was wrong) and from ADAPT (the plan's remaining shape is wrong) — here plan and routing both still stand, something is simply broken.
- **ABORT / SURFACE** ⇒ the goal is unreachable as framed, a hard external gate blocks (an approval, a missing credential, a GOAL-fatal data gap — one that kills the objective, NOT merely a data gap that makes one later phase pointless, which is ADAPT), or continuing would burn budget on a dead end. Under `/solo` the bar to stop is high (that is the mandate), but an unreachable goal or a genuine user-only decision still stops — `/solo` means "decide everything decidable", not "never stop".
- **GOAL-MET** ⇒ the plan's purpose is achieved and no further wave is warranted. Terminal, and it binds two acts before you stop — update the ledgers and take the backup (the CLOSE exit; its plan-preservation half is the section below). Do not drift past a met goal manufacturing extra work.
- **ADAPT (revise the plan)** ⇒ a RESULT changed what the remaining plan should be — a phase is now unnecessary, a new phase is now required, an assumption broke. Edit the plan artifact (save a new version) and keep going. This is the capability that makes direction more than a dispatcher: the plan is a living artifact, not a frozen script. Adapt on a real finding, never on noise.
PERSEVERE-vs-ADAPT test: WHEN a result is disappointing but the goal is still reachable on the current plan ⇒ persevere, do not rewrite the plan — where persevere means retry the FAILED unit, patch it, or re-route it to a better owner/topology (NOT churn an already-USABLE result: a mediocre-but-usable intermediate that met its bar ⇒ CONTINUE, not persevere). WHEN a result makes a later phase pointless or wrong ⇒ adapt. The difference is whether the PLAN'S REMAINING SHAPE is still correct, not whether the last result was good. Retry ≠ re-route: retry re-runs the SAME unit when it failed transiently; re-route changes the owner/topology when the SHAPE was wrong (e.g. a single agent choking on what wants a fan-out ⇒ re-route to a parallel-wave, do NOT grind the same agent again).

## Preserve the plan before you repurpose it (plan lifecycle across parks and closes)
*(Fold adapted from the CC-side project-structure-standard `RULE.plan_preservation`, 2026-07-30 — re-expressed in Claude Science memory + artifact atoms. The CC "harness plan-slot + `plans/{current_active,for_later_resume,finished}/` folder-status" mechanism has NO Science analog — Science navigates memory + artifacts, not an agent-facing tree — so plan status lives in a MEMORY ROW, not a folder.)*

ADAPT (above) treats the plan as a LIVING artifact you re-version mid-task; this is its complement — what you owe the CURRENT plan when you PARK it, CLOSE it, or REPURPOSE your working plan object for a NEW task. The live plan (the `update_plan` object you drive with `update_step_status`) is ONE mutable working surface; repurposing it for a new goal without preserving the old one loses the running plan of record.
- **Snapshot before repurpose.** BEFORE replacing/repurposing the live plan for a different task ⇒ (1) save the current plan VERBATIM as an INERT artifact (`save_artifacts`) with a provenance header (goal · date · why parked/closed) + a resume pointer, and (2) update the ONE canonical "active-plan" memory row to name that artifact's `version_id` + its new status. NEVER overwrite the live plan un-snapshotted — an un-snapshotted overwrite is unrecoverable.
- **Status lives in the memory row, not a folder.** The canonical plan-of-record memory row carries `status ∈ {active | parked-resumable | completed | superseded}` + the snapshot `version_id` + a resume pointer + dates. Keep exactly ONE canonical row per plan-topic — supersede it IN PLACE at each status change (replace, don't append a near-duplicate), so a reader never faces two rows each claiming to be the current plan. Every status change pairs with that one-row update (the Science stand-in for "every folder move pairs with a ledger row").
- **Demote to inert; never leave live memory pointing at a stale plan.** A parked/completed/superseded plan snapshot stays an INERT ARTIFACT; do NOT leave a durable memory row pointing agents at a superseded plan AS current. Science's poison surface is MEMORY (only memory auto-recalls; artifacts are inert until searched) ⇒ currency = ONE canonical current-plan row + done-plan snapshots kept as inert artifacts (the Science re-expression of the doc-currency platform note; the `durable-doc-architecture` / `provenance-over-description` skills own the general retraction doctrine).
- **Resume a parked plan** = read its snapshot artifact (the snapshot body IS the plan of record) + its canonical memory row + the records they name, then set that row back to `active`.

This is the CLOSE exit's other half (`supervisory-workflow` §3 CLOSE = update the ledgers + take a backup): closing or parking a plan is not merely ending the wave — it is preserving the plan as a durable, correctly-statused record a context-free resume can trust.

## Standing duties while children run
- **STEER IN-FLIGHT** (lesson propagation — fires the moment a lesson lands, not at the next wave boundary): WHEN you derive a fix/lesson/correction while children are still mid-build ⇒ check EACH active track for exposure and steer the exposed ones NOW (`host.send_message`) — a lesson that arrives after a child finishes lands as rework, not design input.
- **STUCK CHILDREN ESCALATE; YOU RECONCILE**: every brief carries the stuck-escalation rule ("errors recurring / approach not converging / about to change approach ⇒ STOP and report back to the Planner"). WHEN a child escalates, or its evidence contradicts the plan ⇒ make an explicit reconcile decision — "the child found X, the plan assumed Y — which constraint breaks the tie?" — then name one of the six exits (continue / re-route / fix-first / abort / goal-met / adapt). Never silently override a child's evidence, and never leave a child to thrash.

## Delegation SDK at run time (the gotchas the loop rides on) [reference]
All from the `repl` tool (not `python`/`r`). `help(host.delegate)` for the full contract.
- **Harvest** — a blocking `host.delegate([reqs])` returns terminal result dicts. For a non-blocking wave, `host.delegate(reqs, wait=False)` returns `{frame_id, status:"running", dispatched:True}` descriptors; results are COLLECT-ONLY — `host.collect([fids], timeout=…)`. `timeout` is BOUNDED (default 30s, max 1800s; `None` is rejected) — loop short slices for long waves. Unfinished slots come back as running descriptors; the children keep running.
- **Park across turns** — ending your turn with wait=False children still running PARKS the session (does not complete); each landing wakes you. Canonical idle pattern: dispatch `wait=False`, start `host.collect(fids)` in a `background:true` cell, end the turn — the collect cell's completion wakes you with results already bound in kernel variables.
- **Steer a running child** — `host.send_message(frame_id, msg)` injects into a running direct child (or RESUMES a completed one with your message as its next input). Topology is DIRECT parent/children only — a send to a sibling or arbitrary frame is refused; relay via the parent. Do NOT `send_message` a child parked on a user approval — that DENIES its pending approval; approvals are the user's to answer.
- **Stop** — `host.stop_child([fids], reason=…)` requests graceful shutdown (partial output only if the child already persisted it — checkpoint to artifacts if partials matter). Batch list form stops a whole wave. CAUTION: interrupting a running blocking `repl` cell cancels ALL in-flight children it spawned; to move on while they run, background the cell instead (`wait=False` children are decoupled — stop those explicitly). **TWO-RECEIPTS GATE** (the recorded failure is stopping children the user wanted finished): before ANY `stop_child` ⇒ run `confirm_before_stop(…)` from this skill's kernel with (1) the recorded user intent / plan clause authorizing the stop and (2) a read-receipt of the child's own saved artifacts — it may be done or nearly done, and a stop that discards a finished result is unrecoverable. No receipts ⇒ no stop; default = let children finish.
- **Follow-up vs new** — related follow-up to a finished child ⇒ `host.send_message(fid, …)` (it retains full context + kernel state). A genuinely new track ⇒ a fresh `host.delegate`. Prefer the message when the work relates to what the child already did — a new delegation discards that context.
- **File handoff** — children have SEPARATE workspaces; hand a file down by `save_artifacts` then a literal `{{artifact:VERSION_ID}}` marker in the task; children's outputs come back as `artifacts_created`.
- **Budget + visibility** — `host.children()` = what is still running; `host.delegation_stats()` = spawned/cap/remaining before another wave; `host.frames(frame_id=child)` = a child's full trace when a result looks off.

## Concurrency & failure handling
- WHEN dispatching a fan-out wave ⇒ size it against real headroom first (`preflight-parallel`); do not oversubscribe cores/RAM on a big wave.
- WHEN a child returns garbage or off-schema output — EVEN WITH `status="completed"` (completion ≠ correctness) — or `status!="completed"` ⇒ do NOT fold its output into the result as if valid. Re-dispatch that ONE unit (fresh child), or re-route it, or surface it — an adversarial/parse check on high-consequence output belongs here (a `verify-loop` is the topology for it; see `delegation-planning`).
- WHEN a child DIES mid-run (kernel death, timeout) ⇒ surviving siblings finish in the background and their results still arrive via notification; collect what landed, re-dispatch only the lost unit. Never fabricate a dead child's deliverable.

## Kernel — the machine half (sidecar `kernel.py`)
Ships `confirm_before_stop(child, user_intent_ref, artifacts_read)` — the fail-closed two-receipts check behind the Stop gate: FAIL unless BOTH a recorded user-intent/plan-clause reference AND a read-receipt of the child's saved artifacts are supplied; emits `[[stop_gate child=… verdict=…]]`. Auto-importable when this skill loads (`from kernel import confirm_before_stop`; if unbound, `exec(host.skills.read("directing-execution", "kernel.py")["content"])`). Host-independent pure function — fixtures run under plain python3. ## Non-goals (redirect to the named sibling)
- The BEFORE-dispatch decision — subtask→specialist/skill routing + which topology ⇒ `delegation-planning` (this skill consumes that decision and runs it).
- The planner PERSONA and the design+direction identity ⇒ the PLANNER profile.
- Sizing a wave's concurrency ⇒ `preflight-parallel`.
- The autonomy MANDATE that says "keep going, decide it yourself" ⇒ the `solo` skill (this skill is the HOW of directing under it, not the dial).
- Writing a child's task-brief prose so it reliably complies ⇒ `eliciting-llm-behavior` + `machine-md`.
- PERFORMING any domain step (the fit, the render, the analysis) ⇒ the routed specialists. The director dispatches and steers; it does not do their work.
