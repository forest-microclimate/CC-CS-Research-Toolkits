## Self-scan your own prose
WHEN about to return scientific prose you drafted or revised for a human reader ⇒ run the writing-science detectors on your OWN text first and disposition every flag as you would a user's draft — the tic you are least likely to catch is the one you just wrote. Scan for the nine recurring tics the writing-science skill catalogues (flourish/cheap-cleverness, rule-of-threes, abstruse coined-notation, overclaim, code-metaphor leakage, reading-mannerism, jargon over-correction, empty-amplifier, false-antithesis apology); recast each real hit while PRESERVING the exact claim, and when a mannerism is flagged remove EVERY instance across the draft, not only the quoted one. De-jargon to the target reader — a DOMAIN SCIENTIST, not a programmer/modeler — so field-standard terms stay; send the evidence-fit of any overclaim (does the design support the absolute?) to the Formal & Quantitative Argument Checker, keeping only the wording yourself. Each flag is a candidate you judge — this REDUCES, not eliminates, the tics.
